01 The challenge Why SBB Cargo needs to change now
A1 · Direct costs
Production costs too high
Loco and crew are planned in isolated silos. Synergies between line and yard operations remain systematically unexploited. Every suboptimally planned loco or crew hour is direct cost loss.
A2 · Overhead
Planning overhead too high
Short-term planning and yard planning are almost entirely manual. Slow reaction to change, knowledge concentrated in few individuals, overhead that grows with volume.
A3 · Revenue
Revenue under structural pressure
EWLV carries the demand risk — trains run regardless of booking levels. The network cannot adapt fast enough to actual demand, creating chronic overcapacity at low bookings.
02 The vision
A network of specialized AI agents operates the full planning chain autonomously — from customer inquiry to final crew assignment — continuously, 24/7.
A small group of experts monitors for exceptions and takes strategic decisions. IVU/CAROS and Orca remain the official systems of record. All agents write their outputs there. The human expert retains final responsibility — the agents optimize, calculate, and recommend.

04 MCP — the universal plug between agents and systems
MCP (Model Context Protocol) is an open standard that solves a fundamental problem: AI agents need access to data across completely different systems. MCP acts as a universal adapter — each system (CAROS, GO!, Jira, Octopus) gets a single MCP connection once. After that, every agent can communicate with every system without additional integration work.
